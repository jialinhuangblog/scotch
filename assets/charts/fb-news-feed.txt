FB NEWS FEED — MOCK INTERVIEW SCRIPT
=====================================

這題的瓶頸是寫入放大：一則貼文可能要複製成九千萬份。跟 FB Live Comments 正好
成對，那題是讀端出口頻寬被放大，這題是寫端。最後的做法是混合：一般帳號發文時就把
貼文 id 寫進每個 follower 的 feed，九千萬粉絲的大帳號發文時不展開，讀者打開 feed 時再去現查。佇列跟
60 萬粉絲的門檻都是為這個混合方案設計的。對白英文，場景標題跟導演註解（>）中文。


SCENE 0 — Opening
【現在在做：兩個問題。第一問確定不用排序，第二問確定 fan-out 可以非同步做】
------------------------------------------------------------
INTERVIEWER: Design the Facebook news feed. 45 minutes.

CANDIDATE: First: is the feed ranked, or reverse chronological?

INTERVIEWER: Reverse chronological. No ranking.

CANDIDATE: Good, that removes the ML system and makes this a pure
distribution problem: get the right posts in front of the right followers,
newest first.

Second, and this one decides the architecture: when I post, how quickly
must my followers see it? Is a minute of delay acceptable?

INTERVIEWER: A minute is fine. Why does that decide anything?

CANDIDATE: Because delivering a post to followers is work proportional to
the follower count, and one minute of allowance means that work can happen
asynchronously, on a queue, after the post call has already returned. If
the answer were "instantly," the write path would have to do that work
inline and the whole design gets harder. That one minute is the budget
everything downstream spends.

> 第二個問題拿到「慢一分鐘沒關係」這個答案，fan-out 才能搬到佇列上做。


SCENE 1 — Functional requirements
【現在在做：四條規格，follow 是單向的】
-----------------------------------------
CANDIDATE:

  1. Create posts
  2. Follow other users, one-directional
  3. View a feed of the people I follow, newest first
  4. Page through that feed

  Out of scope: likes, comments, privacy circles, media processing

INTERVIEWER: Why call out one-directional?

CANDIDATE: Because it makes the graph asymmetric, and the asymmetry is the
problem. Friendship caps both sides at the same number. Follow lets one
account have ninety million followers while following twelve people, and
that account is the reason half of this interview exists.

> 候選人用「單向」點出 follow 圖是不對稱的，這是整題困難的來源。九千萬粉絲的
> 帳號在這裡先提出來，SCENE 4 的混合方案就有了前提。


SCENE 2 — Non-functional requirements
【現在在做：量級，再把「可用性優先於一致性」翻成產品語言】
------------------------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE:

  - 2 billion users
  - Posting and feed loading both under 500ms
  - Eventually consistent, up to a minute of staleness on delivery
  - Availability over consistency

That last line translated: if parts of the system disagree for a while, keep
serving. A feed that's missing one recent post is a working product. A feed
that returns an error is not. Nothing here has the "one driver, one ride"
shape from the Uber problem, there is no row two people are fighting over,
so nothing needs to be strongly consistent.

> CAP 的選擇翻成產品語言是：feed 少一則新貼文還能用，回錯誤就不能用。這題連例外都沒有：Uber 有派單那條
> 強一致的路，Tinder 有配對，這題整套都可以 eventual。


SCENE 3 — Data model and API
【現在在做：三張表兩個方向的查詢，跟一個用 timestamp 當游標的分頁】
---------------------------------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE: Three entities: User, Follow, Post. Follow is a row, follower
and followee. Posts partition by author.

The Follow table needs both directions. "Who do I follow" builds my feed.
"Who follows me" delivers my post. One table with the partition key on the
follower side, plus a global secondary index keyed the other way. Same
rows, two query paths.

API:

  POST /posts                          -> postId
  PUT  /users/{id}/follow              idempotent, follow twice = follow once
  GET  /feed?pageSize=20&cursor={ts}   -> posts + next cursor

INTERVIEWER: Why a timestamp cursor instead of page numbers?

CANDIDATE: Because the feed shifts under the reader. With offset paging, if
three new posts arrive while someone reads page one, page two starts with
three posts they already saw. A cursor says "older than this timestamp,"
which stays true no matter how much arrives on top. Offset paging on a
moving list is how you get duplicate posts in every social app's bug
tracker.

> 游標分頁的理由用一個具體 bug 講完：新文插進來，offset 往下推，第二頁開頭
> 重複三則。offset 在這裡首先是正確性問題，其次才是效能問題。


SCENE 4 — Fan-out
【現在在做：feed 的貼文從哪來。寫時展開、讀時現組，最後混合】
--------------------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE: Now the center. When someone opens their feed, where do the
posts come from?

Option one, build at read time: query who I follow, fetch recent posts from
each, merge, sort. I follow 800 people, that's hundreds of partition reads
on the hot path, for every feed load, for 2 billion users. The read fans
out and the 500ms budget dies.

Option two, build at write time: keep a precomputed feed per user, a list
of the 200 newest post IDs. Opening the feed is now one read. Storage: 200
IDs is about 2KB, times 2 billion users is 4 terabytes, large but flat and
cheap. When someone posts, look up their followers via the GSI and prepend
the post ID to each follower's list, through a queue, inside our one-minute
budget.

  200 個 ID × 每個約 10 bytes ≈ 2KB
  2KB × 20 億人 = 40 億 KB = 4TB

INTERVIEWER: And then someone with 90 million followers posts.

CANDIDATE: That's 90 million list-writes for one post. At even 10k writes a
second that's over two hours, the one-minute budget is gone a hundred times
over (150), and the queue is jammed for everyone else. Write-time fan-out is the
right default and it breaks precisely at the accounts people care most
about.
  9,000 萬次寫入 ÷ 10,000 次／秒 = 9,000 秒 = 2.5 小時
  9,000 秒 ÷ 60 秒 = 150 倍

So: hybrid. Accounts over a follower threshold get flagged, their posts
skip fan-out entirely. Reading a feed becomes: take my precomputed list,
separately fetch recent posts from the flagged accounts I follow, merge by
timestamp. Of the 800 accounts I follow, only a handful are huge, so the
read-time work is bounded by that handful.

INTERVIEWER: Where's the threshold?

CANDIDATE: It's a dial, not a constant. The cost curves cross where fan-out
write time exceeds the staleness budget: with 10k queue writes a second,
a minute buys 600k followers, so the flag goes somewhere below that. The
real answer is to measure both paths in production and move the dial; the
design's job is that moving it is a flag flip, not a migration.
  10,000 次／秒 × 60 秒 = 60 萬個粉絲

> 面試官只問了一句 90M，整段結構是候選人自己搭的：先讓兩個純方案各自具體地
> 失敗（幾百次讀 vs 兩小時寫），混合方案才有理由。門檻他說是可以調的旋鈕，
> 再用每秒一萬次寫入乘上一分鐘，推出 60 萬粉絲的上限。


SCENE 5 — The write path in detail
【現在在做：佇列跟 worker，冪等跟截斷這兩個細節】
---------------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE: The fan-out machinery: post lands in the Posts table, a message
goes onto a queue, workers consume it, each message says "post X, deliver
to followers of author Y." Workers page through the follower list via the
GSI and prepend to each precomputed feed.

Two details that matter more than they look.

Idempotent prepend: workers retry, so delivering the same post twice must
leave one copy, not two. A conditional "add if absent" on the list, or
dedup by post ID at read time. Retry plus non-idempotent writes equals
duplicate posts in feeds, which users screenshot.

Truncation: the list holds 200. Prepend then trim. Without the trim, the
precomputed feed of anyone following active accounts grows without bound,
and the 4TB estimate quietly stops being true.

INTERVIEWER: Someone opens their feed mid-fan-out.

CANDIDATE: They see the feed without the newest post, and it arrives within
the minute. That's exactly the staleness we negotiated in scene zero, the
NFR isn't decoration, it's what makes this answer acceptable.

> 冪等跟截斷是這種佇列設計的例行檢查：重試安全嗎、資料會不會無限長大。兩個
> 都用一句「不做會怎樣」收尾。最後一答用的是 SCENE 0 談好的一分鐘延遲。


SCENE 6 — The viral post
【現在在做：讀路徑的熱點：所有人的 feed 都指向同一則貼文】
------------------------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE: The precomputed feed stores IDs, so every feed load hydrates
them into actual posts. Normally that spreads across the Posts table
evenly. Then one post goes viral, and millions of feeds all contain the
same ID, all hydrating against the same partition. Hot key.

A cache in front, obviously, but the shape of the cache matters. A sharded
cache puts each post on exactly one node, so the viral post's traffic all
lands on one shard, we've moved the hot key, not solved it.

Instead: a small replicated cache tier, every node holds the hot set, the
load balancer spreads requests across all of them. A viral post's reads
divide across N nodes instead of concentrating. The price is N cache
misses when a post first turns hot, and N copies in memory, fine, because
the hot set is small, it's the head of the distribution by definition.

> sharded cache 把熱門貼文的流量整個移到另一個節點，replicated cache 才把它
> 分到 N 個節點。這跟 Tinder 的分片方向相反，那題要同一組人落在同一片，這題
> 要同一則貼文散到每一片。


SCENE 7 — Frontend lens
【現在在做：無限捲動的 feed。這題前端的內容比後端還多】
------------------------------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE: This is the richest frontend in the whole set, so I'll take
it properly.

Component tree: a FeedPage owning a Composer and a virtualized FeedList of
PostCards. Virtualization is not optional at feed scale: a morning of
scrolling is thousands of posts, and without windowing the DOM grows until
scrolling stutters. Only the viewport neighborhood exists in the DOM;
images use fixed aspect-ratio placeholders so lazy-loading them doesn't
shift the layout under the reader's thumb.

State split: server state is the feed pages, keyed by cursor, which pairs
naturally with infinite scroll, each page request is just the next cursor.
Client state is the composer draft and scroll position. URL state is the
permalink to a single post; the feed itself deliberately has no URL, an
infinite scroll position isn't shareable, and pretending it is breaks the
back button.

Posting is where this differs from Uber: optimistic works here. The content
is authored by the user, the client isn't guessing a server decision, so
the new post renders in my own feed immediately, marked pending. On
failure, roll it back but keep the draft in the composer, losing a
written post to a network blip is the single most rage-inducing failure a
feed can have.

New posts while reading: don't insert them. A "3 new posts" pill at the
top, announced politely to screen readers, and the reader pulls when
ready. Auto-inserting into a list someone is reading yanks the content
out from under them, same reasoning as the live comments problem, the
reader's position wins over freshness.

Coming back from a permalink: restore the scroll position from the
in-memory feed, don't refetch page one. Losing your place after tapping
into a post is the mobile-web failure everyone knows; keeping the feed
cache warm across that navigation is the fix.

INTERVIEWER: Accessibility beyond the pill?

CANDIDATE: The feed role exists in ARIA for exactly this pattern, it tells
assistive tech "this list mutates," and browsing mode navigates it post by
post. And infinite scroll needs a real "load more" button as the keyboard
fallback, scroll-triggered loading assumes a wheel or a thumb, a keyboard
user tabbing through posts never fires it.

> 這段每一條都有「不做會發生什麼」：DOM 長到捲動卡頓、圖片把版面往下推、
> 回上一頁跳回頂端、鍵盤使用者永遠載不了下一頁。


SCENE 8 — Wrapping up
【現在在做：三個沒展開的】
-----------------------------
CANDIDATE: Three things I'd extend.

Ranking. Reverse-chron made precompute easy because prepend preserves
order. A ranked feed can't prepend, scores change after delivery, so the
precomputed list becomes candidates and a ranker runs at read time. The
hybrid structure survives, the merge gets smarter.

Notifications. Same fan-out machinery, same celebrity problem, different
delivery target. The queue and worker design here is reusable almost
verbatim.

Privacy. Blocked users, restricted posts. The clean enforcement point is
hydration, IDs in a precomputed list are harmless until they turn into
content, so filtering at hydration time means a block takes effect
immediately even for posts already fanned out.

INTERVIEWER: That's time.


跟其他題的差別
================

FB Live Comments 跟這題是同一個問題的兩面：那題一則留言被兩百萬個正在看的
人讀，放大發生在出口頻寬；這題一則貼文被寫進九千萬份 feed，放大發生在寫入
次數。兩題的解法也對稱，那邊用 pub/sub 把留言推進每一條 SSE 連線，這邊用
佇列把九千萬次寫入分批做完。

Uber 的鎖、Tinder 的配對、Bitly 的唯一碼都是必須強一致的部分，這題沒有，
從頭到尾 eventual，難度全部集中在規模而不是正確性。
