TINDER — MOCK INTERVIEW SCRIPT
===============================

技術會、流程不熟的候選人。這題的核心是一個競態，
而且解法跟 Uber 那題的鎖完全不同。
對白英文，場景標題跟導演註解（>）中文。


SCENE 0 — Opening
【現在在做：搞清楚要設計哪一段。候選人又從技術開口】
------------------------------------------------------

INTERVIEWER: Design Tinder. People see other people, swipe left or right, and
when two people both swipe right they match. 45 minutes.

CANDIDATE: Okay, for finding people nearby we'd want Elasticsearch with a geo
index, and then—

INTERVIEWER: You're doing it again. Ask me something first.

CANDIDATE: Right. So the stack of people someone sees — is that filtered by
their preferences, or is it just whoever is nearby?

INTERVIEWER: Filtered. Age range, distance, who they're interested in.

CANDIDATE: If I already swiped on someone, can they show up again later?

INTERVIEWER: No. Never.

CANDIDATE: And after a match — chat, photo upload, super likes?

INTERVIEWER: None of that. Just get them to the match.

> 第二問是這一題最值錢的問題。「滑過的人不能再出現」聽起來像小事，但它是後面
> Bloom filter 存在的唯一理由。如果沒問，Bloom filter 那段就變成沒人要求的功能。


SCENE 1 — Functional requirements
【現在在做：四條規格，二十秒】
-----------------------------------

CANDIDATE:

  1. A user creates a profile with preferences: age range, max distance, who
     they're interested in
  2. A user sees a stack of people matching those preferences
  3. A user swipes right (yes) or left (no)
  4. When two people have both swiped right, both get notified

  Out of scope: chat, photo upload, paid features, fake account detection

INTERVIEWER: That's it.


SCENE 2 — Non-functional requirements
【現在在做：算寫入量，然後決定哪一條需要強一致】
--------------------------------------------------

CANDIDATE: Scale?

INTERVIEWER: Your numbers.

CANDIDATE: Say 20 million daily active users. How many swipes each? People do
this in bursts on a commute — I'll say 100 a day.

20M × 100 = 2 billion swipes a day. Over 86,400 seconds that's about 23,000
writes a second on average. Evening peak is maybe 5x, so 100,000 writes a
second.

INTERVIEWER: Is that a problem?

CANDIDATE: For the write path, it's a lot for a relational database but it's a
very simple write — one row, no joins, no transaction across tables. That's the
shape Cassandra is good at. And I never need to query "all swipes by this user"
in a hurry, so I don't need rich indexes on it.

The read path is the harder one. Building the stack means filtering billions of
profiles by age, distance, and interest, and it has to come back fast enough
that the app doesn't feel stuck. I'd say under 300ms.

> 他把「一億次寫入」拆成「什麼形狀的寫入」。同樣的數字，如果每筆要跨表交易，
> 結論完全不同。這個拆法比報數字有用。

CANDIDATE: One consistency question. If two people swipe right on each other at
the same instant, do we have to catch it?

INTERVIEWER: What happens if you don't?

CANDIDATE: Then neither of them gets a match, and neither of them knows anything
went wrong. They just never hear from each other. That's the worst kind of bug —
silent, and the product's whole reason to exist.

So match detection needs strong consistency. Everything else can be loose: if
someone's stack is slightly stale, or their new photo takes a minute to show up,
nobody suffers.

INTERVIEWER: Agreed.

CANDIDATE: Non-functional requirements:

  - 20M DAU, ~2B swipes/day, ~100k writes/sec at peak
  - Stack loads in under 300ms
  - Match detection: strongly consistent, no missed matches
  - Everything else: eventual is fine
  - A swiped profile never appears again


SCENE 3 — Entities and API
【現在在做：資料模型。Swipe 這張表的形狀等一下會整個改掉】
---------------------------------------------------------

CANDIDATE:

  User    id, name, birthdate, location, prefs { age_min, age_max, distance,
          interested_in }
  Swipe   from_user, to_user, direction (yes | no), created_at
  Match   user_a, user_b, created_at

  POST /profile     { age_min, age_max, distance, interested_in }
  GET  /feed?lat=&lng=&limit=20        -> User[]
  POST /swipe/{userId}   { decision: "yes" | "no" }

INTERVIEWER: Anything you're unsure about there?

CANDIDATE: The Swipe table. Right now it's just a log of who swiped who, and I
suspect the match check is going to force me to change how it's laid out. I'll
come back to it.

> 標記一個自己還沒想清楚的地方，然後繼續走。這比硬講一個自己不確定的設計好，
> 也比停在那裡卡住好。


SCENE 4 — The dumb version
【現在在做：畫最笨的版本，然後自己指出兩個問題】
--------------------------------------------------

CANDIDATE:

  Client → API Gateway → Profile Service → Postgres     (profile, feed)
                       → Swipe Service   → Postgres     (swipes, matches)

  Feed:  SELECT * FROM users
         WHERE age BETWEEN ? AND ?
           AND lat BETWEEN ? AND ? AND lng BETWEEN ? AND ?
           AND interested_in = ?
         LIMIT 20

  Swipe: INSERT INTO swipes ...
         SELECT * FROM swipes WHERE from_user = ? AND to_user = ?  -- 對方滑過我嗎
         if found and both yes: INSERT INTO matches ...

INTERVIEWER: Two problems. Name them.

CANDIDATE: The feed query has the same shape problem as any two-dimensional
range scan — an index on latitude narrows by latitude, then every one of those
rows still has to be checked for longitude, and now also for age and interest.
Across billions of profiles that's a scan, on every swipe, because the stack
runs out every twenty cards.

The second one is the swipe path, and it's worse. Look at the order: insert,
then read, then maybe insert. Three separate operations. Two people swiping at
the same moment both do their insert, then both do their read — and both reads
happen before the other person's insert is visible. Both conclude "they haven't
swiped me," and the match never exists.

INTERVIEWER: Good. Fix the second one.

> 順序：先讓他把兩個問題都講出來，面試官再指定先修哪一個。這比候選人自己決定
> 順序好，因為面試官通常想聽核心那一題。


SCENE 5 — The race
【現在在做：這題的核心。注意解法跟 Uber 的鎖不一樣】
------------------------------------------------------

CANDIDATE: The first instinct is a lock — lock the pair, do the check, release.
That's what I'd reach for.

But I don't think it's right here. A lock costs a round trip on every single
swipe, and there are 100,000 a second. And the thing I'm protecting is tiny: two
rows that are known in advance.

INTERVIEWER: Known in advance how?

CANDIDATE: When A swipes B, the only other write that can conflict is B swiping
A. Nobody else is involved. So instead of locking at runtime, I can decide ahead
of time that those two rows live in the same place, and then use a transaction
that only touches that one place.

In Cassandra that means the partition key is the pair, not the user. Sort the
two ids so both directions produce the same key:

  partition key = min(A,B) + ":" + max(A,B)

  CREATE TABLE swipes (
      user_pair   text,      -- partition key: "123:456"
      from_user   uuid,      -- clustering key
      to_user     uuid,
      direction   text,
      created_at  timestamp,
      PRIMARY KEY ((user_pair), from_user, to_user)
  );

Now "write my swipe and check theirs" is one operation inside one partition, and
Cassandra can make that atomic. The race disappears because the two writes were
never in different places to begin with.

INTERVIEWER: How is this different from the lock you'd use for, say, assigning a
driver to a rider?

CANDIDATE: Because there I don't know who's competing. Any of a hundred riders
might want that driver, and I only find out at runtime, so I need a runtime
mechanism — the lock.

Here the competition is always exactly one known pair. I can design the storage
so they can't collide, and then I don't need to coordinate at all.

> 這一段答完就贏了。「事先知道誰會競爭 → 用資料佈局；事後才知道 → 用鎖」是可以
> 帶去別的題目的判準，而且他是被問到才講出來的。

INTERVIEWER: What's the cost?

CANDIDATE: Hot partitions. Someone who gets swiped by a hundred thousand people
has a hundred thousand pair-partitions, which is fine — but a very active
swiper's own rows are spread across just as many partitions, so "show me my
swipe history" becomes a scatter query. I've optimised for the write and the
match check, and made history reads worse. That's the trade I'd accept, because
history is a rare screen and the match check is every swipe.

Alternative: keep recent swipes in Redis and do the atomic check with a Lua
script, flushing to Cassandra for durability. Faster, but now I have two stores
and a window where they disagree.


SCENE 6 — The feed
【現在在做：讀路徑。一次給一層，等面試官問「夠嗎」】
-------------------------------------------------------

CANDIDATE: Back to the stack. The filter is geo plus age plus interest, so it's
a multi-field search, not just a geo lookup. That's what a search index is for —
Elasticsearch, with the profiles denormalized into it.

INTERVIEWER: How does it stay in sync with the user database?

CANDIDATE: Change data capture. The user table is the source of truth, changes
stream out of it and update the index. It lags by seconds, which is fine — a
profile edit showing up a few seconds late costs nothing.

INTERVIEWER: Is a query per stack fast enough?

CANDIDATE: Probably, but I'd rather not find out during peak. Most people open
the app and swipe through a stack immediately, so I can precompute: a background
job builds a stack for active users and caches it. Opening the app is then a
cache read.

INTERVIEWER: What breaks?

CANDIDATE: Staleness. Someone changes their distance setting, or travels, and
their cached stack is for the old criteria. So a short TTL, under an hour, plus
an explicit invalidation when preferences or location change.

And heavy users exhaust the cached stack. When they're down to the last few
cards, the client asks for more and that request goes live to Elasticsearch. So
the cache covers the common opening, and the index covers the tail.

> 三層：搜尋索引 → 預先算好放快取 → 快取用完打索引。每一層都是被追問之後才給的。


SCENE 7 — Never show the same person twice
【現在在做：兌現 SCENE 0 問到的那條需求】
--------------------------------------------

INTERVIEWER: You said a swiped profile never comes back. How?

CANDIDATE: The naive version is to check the swipe table while building the
stack. Someone who's been swiping for three years has hundreds of thousands of
rows, and with the pair-partition layout that check is a scatter query. Too slow
to do while assembling twenty cards.

Client-side helps a bit: the app keeps the last few hundred swipes locally and
filters them out. But it doesn't cover the long tail.

So for heavy users I'd keep a Bloom filter per user, cached. Ask it "have I
swiped this person," and it answers with one property that matters here: it
never says no when the answer is yes. It can occasionally say yes when the
answer is no.

INTERVIEWER: What does that mean for the product?

CANDIDATE: A false positive means someone who I've never seen gets silently
skipped. They just never appear in my stack. That's invisible to both people and
costs nothing — there are millions of other profiles. A false negative would be
showing someone I already rejected, which users notice and complain about. The
error the filter can make is the harmless one, which is why it fits.

> 這個回答的形狀值得記：不是解釋 Bloom filter 怎麼運作，是說明它會犯的錯剛好是
> 這個產品付得起的錯。面試官要聽的是後者。


SCENE 8 — Frontend lens
【現在在做：主動講前端。手勢那條是這題獨有的】
------------------------------------------------

CANDIDATE: A few minutes on the client, because this UI has decisions the
backend can't make for it.

First, prefetching. The stack is twenty cards but the photos are the payload. I'd
load the next two or three images ahead of the current card — swipe to a white
box and the whole feel is gone. And I'd refill the stack when three cards remain,
not when it empties, so the fetch happens while the user is still busy.

Second, what happens when a swipe fails. The card is already gone from the screen
— the animation ran, the user moved on. Putting it back would be worse than
losing the swipe: they already made the decision and seeing that face return
reads as a bug. So the POST retries quietly in the background and the card stays
gone. That's a deliberate choice to accept a lost write, and I'd write it down
rather than let it be an accident.

Third, accessibility, and this one is specific to a swipe interface. A drag
gesture doesn't exist for someone using a screen reader or a switch device.
Like and pass have to be real buttons — not a fallback tucked in a menu, the
primary control, with the gesture as the shortcut for people who can use it.
And when the card changes, the new person's name and details need to be
announced, otherwise the screen reader user has no idea the deck moved.

Fourth, DOM size. Only the top three cards render; the rest of the stack is data,
not elements. A hundred cards of images in the tree makes every animation stutter.

INTERVIEWER: The button-first framing is the right way round.

> 第三點是這一整份腳本的差異化。多數候選人會說「加上按鈕當備援」，說成「按鈕是
> 主要操作，手勢是捷徑」是另一個層級。


SCENE 9 — Wrapping up
【現在在做：列出沒講的，說明為什麼是這三個】
-----------------------------------------------

INTERVIEWER: What's missing?

CANDIDATE: Three.

Ranking. I've been treating the stack as "anyone who matches the filter," but
who appears first is the actual product. That's a scoring system on top of the
same retrieval.

Fake profiles and abuse. It changes the write path — a swipe from a bot should
never reach the match logic — and it's a whole detection system.

And the unmatch and block path. Both of those have to remove people from stacks
retroactively, which means the Bloom filter and the cached stacks both need an
eviction story I haven't designed.

INTERVIEWER: Good. Time.
