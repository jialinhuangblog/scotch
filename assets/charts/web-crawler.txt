WEB CRAWLER — MOCK INTERVIEW SCRIPT
====================================

這題沒有使用者。客戶端是我們自己，別人的伺服器才是要保護的對象，所以「可用性」
整個反過來讀。NFR 也不是 QPS，是一個死線：10 億是別題的用戶數，這題是頁數，
而且要在五天內抓完。對白英文，場景標題跟導演註解（>）中文。


SCENE 0 — Opening
【現在在做：先確認這是一個批次工作，不是一個線上服務】
--------------------------------------------------------
INTERVIEWER: Design a web crawler. 45 minutes.

CANDIDATE: First question: is this a one-shot crawl with a finish line, say
building a training corpus, or a continuously running service that keeps
pages fresh, like a search engine's crawler?

INTERVIEWER: One-shot. We're collecting text to train a model. 10 billion
pages, and we'd like it done in about 5 days.

CANDIDATE: That changes the whole shape. A one-shot job is a pipeline with a
deadline, so the math I need to do is bandwidth math, not QPS math. And
freshness, re-crawling, scheduling by change frequency, all of that drops
out of scope.

Second question: how seriously do we take politeness? robots.txt, per-domain
rate limits?

INTERVIEWER: Non-negotiable. We don't want angry emails from ten thousand
webmasters.

CANDIDATE: Good, because politeness is the part that makes the queue design
interesting. A crawler that ignores it is a DDoS with a mission statement.

> 「一次性還是持續性」是這題的第一個岔路，答案直接砍掉一半的題目範圍。先問
> 這個再動筆，比畫完才被問「那 re-crawl 呢」好看得多。


SCENE 1 — Functional requirements
【現在在做：規格很短，範圍切割才是重點】
------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE:

  1. Start from seed URLs, crawl outward through links
  2. Extract the text of each page and store it
  3. Respect robots.txt and per-domain rate limits

  Out of scope: JavaScript-rendered pages, pages behind login, images and
  video, and the model training itself. Input is a seed list, output is
  text sitting in blob storage.

INTERVIEWER: Fine.

> FR 只有三條，但第三條放進 FR 而不是 NFR 是刻意的：禮貌不是品質屬性，是這個
> 系統「做什麼」的一部分，違反它系統就是錯的，跟漏抓一頁不是同一等級。


SCENE 2 — Non-functional requirements
【現在在做：把五天死線換算成機器數，這段算完架構規模就定案了】
--------------------------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE: Let me turn the deadline into machine counts.

We fetch HTML only, images and video are out of scope, and an HTML document
transfers a few tens of kilobytes on average. The tail is long, so call it
100KB. 10 billion pages at 100KB is about 1 petabyte over the wire in
5 days, around 2.3 gigabytes a second sustained. A single network-optimized
instance gives around 200 Gbps on paper, so bandwidth does not size this
fleet at all.

Concurrency does. 10 billion over 5 days is about 23,000 pages a second,
and a fetch spends most of its life waiting: DNS, TLS handshake, a slow
server on the other end. At an average of two seconds per fetch that is
46,000 requests in flight at any moment. One big instance holds maybe
8,000 concurrent connections before file descriptors and memory get
uncomfortable, which is 4,000 pages a second per machine. Six machines
covers the rate; eight gives slack for retries and for a machine dying
mid-crawl. So the fetch fleet is eight-ish big instances, not hundreds of
small ones, and the design should assume a machine dies and nothing is
lost.

  - 10B pages, ~5 days: ~23k pages/sec needed, fleet sized to ~30k
  - Fault tolerant: progress survives any single machine dying
  - Polite: robots.txt honored, bounded rate per domain
  - Efficient: don't fetch the same content twice

INTERVIEWER: So what's actually slow inside a single fetch?

CANDIDATE: Surprisingly often it's DNS. Every new hostname is a resolution,
and if you don't cache, resolution can eat most of the elapsed time per
URL. I'll give DNS its own treatment: an aggressive local cache, and
spreading queries across several providers so we don't rate-limit ourselves
at the resolver. It's unglamorous and it's worth more than another fetcher.

> 「只抓 HTML，一頁抓 100KB，頻寬一張網卡就夠；機器數來自同時連線：一頁等兩
> 秒，隨時掛著 46,000 條」。這題的 NFR 段跟別題不同，算的不是尖峰流量而是
> 總量除以死線。DNS 那句是實戰訊號，沒抓過的人想不到瓶頸會在解析。


SCENE 3 — The pipeline
【現在在做：兩段式管線，中間用 S3 隔開，理由是重試的成本差一百倍】
--------------------------------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE: The skeleton is a two-stage pipeline with a queue in front of
each stage.

Frontier queue holds URLs to fetch. Fetchers pull a URL, download the HTML,
and write the raw bytes straight to S3, unparsed. Then a message goes onto
a second queue, and extractor workers pull from that, parse the HTML,
extract the text into S3, and extract the links.

INTERVIEWER: Why not parse right in the fetcher? One stage, no second
queue.

CANDIDATE: Because the two stages fail differently and cost differently to
retry. A fetch retry costs network, politeness budget, and another hit on
someone's server. A parse retry costs CPU on bytes we already own. If a
parser bug surfaces on day 3, and parser bugs on weird HTML are a
certainty, I re-run parsing over S3 at full speed without touching a single
website again. Fused into one stage, that bug means re-crawling.

Storing raw HTML first also means the corpus can be re-processed later for
something else, links to images, structured data, whatever, without a new
crawl.

INTERVIEWER: A fetcher dies holding fifty URLs.

CANDIDATE: The queue's visibility timeout covers that. A message being
worked on is invisible, not deleted; the fetcher deletes it only after the
HTML is safely in S3. Machine dies, timeout expires, the URL reappears and
another fetcher takes it. Retries come free from the same mechanism, and
backoff is just lengthening the visibility timeout on each failure. After
about five failed attempts the message moves to a dead-letter queue so one
permanently broken URL can't clog the pipeline, and we can look at the DLQ
later to see what kind of pages we're systematically failing on.

> 「兩段拆開」的理由講到了錢：fetch 重試花的是網路跟禮貌額度，parse 重試花的
> 是自己的 CPU。visibility timeout 這段是 SQS 的正確用法：不是自己做 ack 跟
> retry，是指出佇列本身就內建了這套。


SCENE 4 — Politeness
【現在在做：robots.txt 跟每個域名的 rate limit，這是這題的良心，也是排程變難的原因】
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE: Politeness has two halves: what a site allows, and how fast we
hit it.

robots.txt: fetched once per domain, cached in the metadata DB with the
domain record. Before fetching any URL we check the cached rules,
disallowed paths get dropped, and a Crawl-delay directive overrides our
default rate for that domain.

Rate: default one request per second per domain. Implementation is a
per-domain lock in Redis, SET NX with a one-second TTL. Fetcher grabs the
lock, fetches; if the lock is taken, the URL isn't dropped, it goes back to
the queue with a delay.

INTERVIEWER: What happens when the frontier is full of one domain? Say a
million URLs from the same site cluster together.

CANDIDATE: That's the real cost of politeness: at one per second, a domain
caps out at 86,400 pages a day, roughly 430 thousand inside the five-day
window, and a million pages from one domain takes eleven days no matter how
many fetchers I own. The fleet math from earlier silently assumed no single
domain exceeds that cap; I should say it out loud. Sites bigger than that
end the crawl partially fetched, and for a training corpus I accept it,
breadth across domains is worth more than completeness of any one site. If
one giant site is a must-have, the lever is raising that single domain's
rate where its robots.txt allows it, not adding machines.

The deadline holds because the load spreads across millions of domains.
When a burst of same-domain URLs does cluster, fetchers keep
pulling them, failing to get the lock, and re-queueing with delay, which
wastes queue throughput. Adding jitter to the re-queue delay spreads them
out so they don't all come back in the same second. It's the same lesson as
retry storms in the rate limiter problem, from the other side of the wall.

> 「一百萬頁 × 每秒一次 = 十一天，加機器也沒用」把禮貌的代價變成一個算得出來
> 的數字。能把 rate limiter 題的 jitter 教訓搬過來用，是讀過系統而不是背過
> 題目的訊號。


SCENE 5 — Dedup and traps
【現在在做：兩層去重（URL 跟內容），然後給爬蟲陷阱設一個深度上限】
--------------------------------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE: Two dedup layers, because two different things repeat.

URL level: before a link goes into the frontier, a conditional write into
the metadata DB, insert if not seen. The DB is the source of truth for
"have I queued this," so a crashed extractor that re-emits links doesn't
double-queue them.

Content level: the same page often lives at many URLs, mirrors, tracking
parameters, http versus https. Hash the extracted text, check the hash
before storing. Two options for the check: an indexed column in the
metadata DB, exact but one more DB round-trip per page, or a Bloom filter
in Redis, faster and smaller but with false positives, and a false
positive here means silently dropping a page we never actually saw. For a
training corpus a tiny drop rate is acceptable, so the Bloom filter is
defensible; for an archive it wouldn't be.

INTERVIEWER: And crawler traps?

CANDIDATE: Pages that generate infinite links, calendars that link to the
next day forever, session IDs that make every URL unique. The blunt,
effective guard is a depth limit: track how many hops a URL is from the
seed, drop anything past 15 or 20. Legitimate content lives shallow;
nothing worth training on is 40 links deep into a calendar.

> Bloom filter 的取捨講法跟 Tinder 題同款但方向相反：Tinder 是「不會漏、會誤
> 殺」拿去擋滑過的人，這裡是「誤判等於默默丟頁」，所以要先問這個語料掉幾頁
> 有沒有關係，才知道能不能用。


SCENE 6 — Frontend lens
【現在在做：爬蟲的前端是操作員的儀表板，設計對象是凌晨三點被叫起來的人】
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
CANDIDATE: No consumer ever sees this system, but somebody operates it for
five straight days, so the frontend is the operator dashboard, and its user
is a person who just got paged.

The first widget answers one question: are we on pace? Pages per second,
fleet-wide, against the line we need to finish in 5 days. One number
against one target. Everything else, queue depths, error rates by domain,
DLQ size, exists to explain that number when it sags.

Data fetching is plain polling. This is minute-granularity aggregate data;
a 30-second poll is fine, and the tab stops polling when hidden. No
WebSocket, nothing here is a conversation.

The DLQ table is where actual work happens: the operator reads failure
samples to spot systematic problems, one domain serving garbage, one
parser crash signature. That table needs virtualization once it holds tens
of thousands of rows, keyboard navigation, and filters that live in the
URL, so the person who finds "all failures are domain X" can paste the
exact filtered view into the incident channel and the next person opens
the same thing.

Controls: pause a domain, retry a DLQ batch. Those are POSTs that flip
state in the metadata DB, and they get confirmation dialogs, at 3am the
difference between "pause domain" and "pause crawl" should take two
clicks to cross, not a slip of one.

a11y carries over from the ad click dashboard: the pace chart gets a text
line stating current rate versus needed rate, tables are real tables, and
counters that tick every second are aria-live off, a screen reader
announcing queue depth once per second is unusable.

> 前端這段的自我定位：使用者是操作員而不是消費者，於是每個選擇都跟著改。
> 「篩選條件進 URL，貼給下一個值班的人」是把 URL state 用在這題真正需要的
> 地方。


SCENE 7 — Wrapping up
【現在在做：三個沒展開的】
-----------------------------
CANDIDATE: Three things I'd extend.

Continuous crawling. Turn the finish line into a loop: a scheduler that
re-queues pages by how often they historically change. That's a different
frontier design, priority instead of FIFO.

JavaScript rendering. A headless browser fleet costs an order of magnitude
more per page, so you'd want a classifier that decides which pages deserve
it instead of rendering everything.

Being a good citizen at scale. A published crawler name, contact info in
the user agent, honoring 429s from sites, and an easy way for a webmaster
to opt out. At 30 thousand pages a second, courtesy is an operational
requirement, the alternative is getting our IP ranges blocked mid-crawl.

INTERVIEWER: That's time.


跟其他題的差別
================

別題的可用性是「我要活著服務別人」，這題是「別人要活著被我抓」：唯一會被流量
影響的是目標網站，所以 rate limiter 裝在自己身上。rate limiter 題的機制搬過來了三個：
rate limit、退避、jitter，只是方向反過來。

NFR 的形狀也不同：沒有 QPS、沒有 p99，只有一個總量除以死線。算數從「尖峰每秒
多少」變成「100 億頁五天內抓完要幾台」，而且決定機器數的不是頻寬是同時連線數：
一頁 100KB，一張網卡的頻寬就夠，貴的是每次抓都掛著連線等兩秒。
